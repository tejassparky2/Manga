## The research in short (as the video session summarised it)
- The best-loved games have one-card rules and a calm all-survive reading on the page from the start; people break it.
- Explainable "within 30 seconds"; readers watch the people, not the rulebook.
- Readers punish withheld information: a rule revealed only when it kills, a host breaking its rules, an off-screen
  plan, a hero who never loses, smart people turning stupid, luck deciding deaths, a pointless sacrifice, a twist
  that cancels the story, a bloc vote instead of thinking, long explanation dumps.
- Betrayal hurts when trust was earned on screen first; it goes stale if every ally betrays.
- A reveal should re-read the past (one flip line, two or three flashback panels, one proof card).
- Rotate the weakness and the loophole kind from game to game.

## Sixteen rules (each sourced in the report)
1. One card: 3–5 one-sentence rules plus one worked example with real numbers.
2. The exit is written into the card (a word, a missing word, two rules read together, an object on the page).
3. At least two obvious answers fail on the page, for reasons the rules give.
4. Never a new rule. Momo may rule on a reading only before anyone relies on it.
5. Show the key clue twice before it's used, first innocently.
6. Plant the betrayer's motive at least one part before the betrayal.
7. Rotate the weakness and the loophole kind.
8. Ren pays something at least every other game; his reasoning is on the page, one step per panel.
9. Equal opponents: Riku wins through a flaw Ren has shown, and loses through a flaw of his own already shown.
10. Momo obeys every rule it states; if it bends one, a character says so.
11. Luck may set things up; a choice decides every death.
12. A voting bloc can be the threat, never the solution; no move removes a player's ability to act.
13. Death is a ritual, a number or an empty seat, never anatomy.
14. Reveal = one flip line, two or three flashbacks, a proof card; no explanation over two lines without a new image.
15. Twists re-read the past, never cancel it.
16. Write the truth sheet first.

## A. THE HATCH (an object; broken by revenge against a debt). Suggested Game 4.
Rules: (1) The richest player waits alone in Room B; everyone else in Room A. (2) One key, in Room A; fits both
doors; turns once, then breaks. (3) Room A votes; the key works only in the door most choose. (4) In ten minutes,
anyone not through the exit at the end of the corridor dies.
Set-up: Riku (60) alone in B; 17 in A (odd, no tie). A small hatch between the rooms passes objects.
Wrong answers: "Open A. He killed Saki."; "Open B, and we're locked in."; "Break the door." (Momo: "Doors are very strong!")
Loophole: the floor plan on Room A's wall shows A's door is held by one bolt on the corridor side. Vote B, pass the
key through the hatch, Riku unlocks B, walks the corridor and slides A's bolt. Everyone out.
Proof card: KEY → ROOM B · RIKU OUT · A'S BOLT IS OUTSIDE → A OPEN · 18 OUT.
Breaks (author's choice): the vote goes A and Riku dies before anyone reads the bolt; or B passes by one and Riku
stands at the bolt naming his price ("You owe me twice"), or Momo whispers him an offer (only if announced on the card).
Clue twice: the floor plan from the first page; Aoi: "Every bolt in this place is on the outside."
Built from: the trust game (Berg, Dickhaut and McCabe); a majority vote that makes "who dies" a choice everyone owns.
Alternative: "The player Momo paid waits alone in Room B" (Haruto); darker but less balanced.

## B. THE LAST HUNT (a commitment; broken by fear or a fake tear)
Rules: (1) Momo draws nine pairs. (2) Everyone gets two cards, STAG and HARE. (3) At the bell, the card in your hand
is your choice; empty hands or both cards count as HARE. (4) Both STAG: both walk. (5) One HARE: the HARE walks and
the STAG dies. Both HARE: both die.
Wrong answers: everyone promises STAG (costs nothing); everyone holds HARE (a HARE pair dies); hold both (counts as HARE).
Loophole: nothing forbids destroying your own card. Tear your HARE in front of your partner; when both have torn, the pair walks.
Breaks: fear (one won't tear, both hold HARE, both die); a fake tear (card back hidden).
Clue twice: STAG backs green, HARE backs red when dealt; the betrayer's torn pieces are green.
Built from: the stag hunt plus the "burned steering wheel" commitment.

## C. THE ROLL CALL (order; broken by the temptation of power)
Rules: (1) Everyone votes for one classmate, one at a time, out loud, in any order. (2) Most votes dies. (3) A tie
for most: nobody dies. (4) No voting for yourself.
Wrong answers: vote in a circle (the last voter gets a free kill); nobody votes last (someone must); the most trusted votes last (still a free kill).
Loophole: make the last voter the leader by exactly one (2 votes; 15 others at 1, 2 at 0). His only safe vote makes
a tie at 2, so nobody dies; self-interest forces mercy.
Breaks: the second-to-last voter can kill the last voter with a third vote; a stray vote hands the last voter a free kill.
Clue twice: Haruto jokes "I'll just vote for myself" (Momo: "Never yourself!"); rule 3 read aloud twice.
Built from: the pirate game's backward induction; The Genius's elimination by nomination.

## D. THE PLATE (wording; broken by panic and distrust of the maths). At least three games after the Charity.
Rules: (1) When time runs out, the plate in the floor must be pressed down, or everyone dies. (2) It takes 40 kg.
(3) Anyone touching the plate when time runs out dies.
Room: twenty folding chairs, "one for each student of Class 3-B!", including Saki's and Gō's; every leg stamped 4 KG.
Loophole: rule 1 never says "by a player". 20 chairs × 4 kg = 80 kg; stack them on the plate.
Breaks: no gauge; the stack wobbles at ten seconds; a frightened student jumps on "to make sure" and dies.
Clue twice: the 4 KG stamp in the first wide shot and again later; "Twenty chairs! One for everybody!"
Built from: the volunteer's dilemma plus exact wording (same family as the Charity, hence the spacing).

## E. THE ORACLE (a prediction; broken by greed and pride in logic). Late; its clue must be planted first.
Rules: (1) One at a time, enter the booth alone; nobody who comes out may speak to anyone waiting. (2) Inside: a glass
box with 10 tokens, and a black box. (3) Take the black box only, or both. (4) Last night the Algorithm predicted each
choice; "black only" predicted → the black box holds an exit pass; "both" → empty. (5) No pass, no exit.
Wrong answers: Aoi's "the boxes are already filled" argument; take both and return the tokens; take neither.
Loophole: the Algorithm's record. If it has never been wrong, everyone takes only the black box and walks.
Breaks: greed in a broke class; pride in logic (the one who dies was right about the boxes, wrong about the predictor).
Clue: from Game 4 on, every result board shows "PREDICTED SURVIVORS" beside the real count, always matching. It must not
explain the meteor (bible mystery rules).
Built from: Newcomb's problem.

## Checklist before any game goes to the author (video session's, abridged)
Rules repeatable after one read · every rule the ending uses shown before the midpoint · key clue shown twice ·
solution follows from the card alone · two or more obvious answers fail on the page · the all-survive path has exact
numbers · the breaker's weakness planted a part earlier · weakness and loophole kind rotated · the betrayal works
without anyone turning stupid · luck only sets things up · Momo obeys its rules · no move removes someone's ability to
act · the twist re-reads two earlier panels · reveal at most one flip line, two or three flashbacks, one proof card ·
Ren lost or paid something here or last game · a loved character still at risk · no gore · the five "why didn't they
just..." questions answered on the page · not recognisable as a famous game (0.8 × average, minority rule, split or
steal, limited-card RPS, glass bridge, hide-and-seek, musical chairs, marbles, red light green light, symbol collars).
