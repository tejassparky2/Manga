## 2. GAME 03 — THE VERDICT: the rules

**The room:** round, with 20 pedestals in a circle, built for the whole class. One pedestal stays dark. Each pedestal has one ballot card and one slot. A token dropped into the slot with your hand inside can't be seen. The screen reads PLAYERS: 19. Ten minutes.

1. Hand in one ballot naming one name: another player, never yourself. No ballot, and you die.
2. You may add tokens when you hand in your ballot. Each token is one more vote for that name, and it's gone. Then your slot locks.
3. When time runs out, the player with the most votes dies.
4. If two or more players tie for the most votes, nobody dies.
5. Nobody can take your tokens. You may give them away.

Each rule closes an escape route a reader would ask about:
- **Rule 1** blocks abstaining, voting for Momo (Game 2 settled that Momo isn't a player) and voting for yourself.
- **Rule 2** makes Riku's 69 tokens deadly. It also means nobody can react after their own ballot is in.
- **Rule 4** looks like mercy, but it's the hidden way out.
- **Rule 5** means strength can't settle it (bible 3.6), and even a found token couldn't be taken.

The rules never say "eliminated", so Q6 (whether eliminated means executed) stays open.

## 3. The logic

### Riku's power, and the class's counter-threat (exact)
- Riku puts all 69 tokens on one name, giving it 70 votes. All 18 others together make 18. **He chooses who dies.**
- If all 18 write "Riku", Riku has 18 votes, and he has three choices:

| Riku adds to the name on his card | That name | Riku | Result | Riku keeps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 or fewer | 17 or fewer | 18 | **Riku dies** | 53+ |
| 17 | 18 | 18 | tie, nobody dies | 52 |
| 18 or more | 19 or more | 18 | **that player dies** | 51 or fewer |

- So the threat costs Riku 17 tokens just to stay alive. Ren uses that cost as leverage: "Seventeen games after this one. You'll want those tokens."

### Three obvious answers fail on the page
1. **"Everyone writes Riku"** (Gō). Riku either pays 17 tokens for a tie or 18 to kill. The class's lives still depend on what he chooses.
2. **"Nobody hands anything in"** (Haruto). Rule 1 kills everyone who doesn't.
3. **"Pair up and vote for your partner"** (Mio). 19 is 9 pairs and one left over. Nobody will pair with Riku, so he's the one left over. Whoever he names gets 2 votes and dies. *Saki's death made the class odd.*

### The real answer: combine Rule 1 and the words "or more" in Rule 4
Everyone names the person on their left in the circle, skipping the dark pedestal.
- 19 ballots. 19 names. Each name appears exactly once.
- So every player has exactly 1 vote.
- "The most votes" is 1, and all 19 players have it.
- 19 is "two or more". Rule 4: nobody dies.

**Why Riku accepts:** in the circle, the cheapest way for Riku to live is to kill nobody. It costs him 0 tokens, against 17 under the threat. Ren turns Riku's selfishness into everyone's safety.

**Clues visible before Ren says it:** the round room; Rule 4 says "two **or more**"; Ren solved Game 2 by rereading one word.

**What the circle requires:** not one extra vote anywhere. Everyone shows their card to the person they named before putting it in, which stops anyone writing a different name. That leaves one thing nobody can check: **tokens in the slot.**

### How it breaks: Riku's one token
Riku can't account for Saki's token, so he adds exactly one token to his own ballot. His ballot names Gō, the player on his left. The hidden token can only go on its holder's own card, and no one but Riku names Gō.

| If the hidden token… | Riku adds 0 | Riku adds 1 (what he did) | Riku adds 2 or more |
|---|---|---|---|
| goes on Riku's name | Riku 2 → **Riku dies** | Riku 2, Gō 2 → tie, nobody dies | Gō 3+ → **Gō dies** |
| goes on someone else (Y) | Y 2 → **Y dies** | Y 2, Gō 2 → tie, nobody dies | Gō 3+ → **Gō dies** |
| isn't used | everyone 1 → nobody dies | Gō 2 → **Gō dies** | Gō 3+ → **Gō dies** |

- **What one token does:** it's the smallest amount that keeps Riku alive in every case. It's also the only amount under which a used hidden token kills nobody. It's the same tie trick Riku described aloud earlier ("Eighteen and eighteen is a tie").
- **Its price:** Gō dies, but only if the class stays honest.
- **The dark core:** Kota's mercy killed Gō. Kota's revenge would have saved him.

## 7. Reader questions, answered

1. **Why not vote for Momo, like Game 2?** Rule 1 says "another player". Aoi points it out (7.13).
2. **Why not abstain?** Rule 1: no ballot, and you die.
3. **Why can't Gō just take Riku's tokens?** Rule 5.
4. **Why can't the class outvote Riku?** 70 votes against 18.
5. **If all 18 write Riku and he pays 17 for a tie, nobody dies. Why not do that?** It costs Riku 17 tokens, and the class's lives still depend on whether he pays 17 or 18. The circle asks no tokens from anyone, which is why Riku accepts it.
6. **Why does the circle work?** Everyone has 1 vote, and 19 players tie (section 3).
7. **Why do pairs fail but a circle works?** Pairs need an even number. A circle works with any number.
8. **Couldn't someone write a different name than agreed?** Each pedestal has one card, and everyone shows theirs to the person they named. The only thing no one can see is tokens.
9. **Why did Riku let them search him?** To clear himself. He showed exactly 69, so he didn't have Saki's token.
10. **Why didn't anyone check Kota's notebook?** They turned out pockets, not books. Kota always clutches that notebook, ever since Riku took it in Chapter 1. Then Ren stopped the search (9.10).
11. **Why didn't the holder return the token when nobody could see?** Kota wanted it for revenge. Choosing to keep it is what makes the betrayal clue fair (9.13–9.14).
12. **Why didn't Riku give his tokens away?** Fear: they're his only protection from 18 people who hate him. He also said he'd never walk into the next game with nothing.
13. **Why did Riku go last?** So nobody could react to his ballot. Rule 2 locks every slot after its ballot goes in.
14. **Why exactly one token?** See the case table in section 3. It's the same tie logic Riku stated in 8.4.
15. **Why did the token hit Gō and not Ren, whom Riku threatened?** A token only adds to the name on your own card. Riku's card had to name the person on his left, and Gō chose that spot to keep Yui out of it.
16. **Why didn't Riku warn anyone?** He trusts numbers, not people ("I trusted a number", Game 1). Going last means no one can respond.
17. **Why didn't Kota use the token?** Saki's "He's still one of us", plus certain exposure: only his card named Riku.
18. **Couldn't Ren have stopped it?** He worked it out at 00:06. Every slot except Riku's was already locked (9.31), so no one could act.
19. **Did anyone lie?** No one broke a promise. Riku never agreed to add no tokens (9.15–9.16). Kota only kept quiet.
20. **How does Ren know what happened?** Only Riku's card named Gō, so the second vote had to be a token on Riku's card. Riku shows 68.
21. **Why is Rule 4 kind when Game 1 punished ties?** Different game, different rules. Haruto assumes ties are deadly; Aoi reads the actual rule.
22. **How does Gō "die"?** The same way as Saki: lights out, then gone. Nothing new is revealed.
