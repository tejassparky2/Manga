## 2. GAME 03 — THE VERDICT: the rules

**The room:** round, with 20 pedestals in a circle, built for the whole class. One pedestal stays dark. Each pedestal has one ballot card and one slot. A token dropped into the slot with your hand inside can't be seen by anyone. The screen reads PLAYERS: 19. Ten minutes.

1. Hand in one ballot naming one name: another player, never yourself. No ballot, and you die.
2. You may add tokens to your ballot. Each token is one more vote for that name, and it's gone.
3. When time runs out, the player with the most votes dies.
4. If two or more players tie for the most votes, nobody dies.
5. Nobody can take your tokens. You may give them away.

Each rule closes an escape route a reader would ask about:
- **Rule 1** blocks abstaining and voting for Momo ("another player"; Game 2 settled that Momo isn't one). It also blocks voting for yourself.
- **Rule 2** makes Riku's 69 tokens frightening.
- **Rule 4** looks like mercy, but it's the hidden way out.
- **Rule 5** stops anyone, Gō included, from simply grabbing Riku's tokens. It also makes searching for the missing token pointless: even if they found it, they couldn't take it.

The rules never say "eliminated", so Q6 (whether eliminated means executed) stays open.

## 3. The loophole

### Three obvious answers fail on the page first
1. **"Everyone writes Riku"** (Gō). Riku adds 69 tokens to one name, giving that name 70 votes. All 18 others together give 18. *He chooses who dies.*
2. **"Nobody hands anything in"** (Haruto). Rule 1 kills everyone who doesn't.
3. **"Pair up and vote for your partner"** (Mio). 19 is odd: 9 pairs and one left over. Nobody will pair with Riku, so he's the one left over. Whoever he names gets 2 votes and dies. *Saki's death made the class odd.*

### The real answer: combine Rule 1 and the words "or more" in Rule 4
Everyone names the person on their left in the circle, skipping the dark pedestal.

**Proof, one step per line:**
- 19 ballots. 19 names. Each name appears exactly once.
- So every player has exactly 1 vote.
- "The most votes" is 1, and all 19 players have it.
- 19 is "two or more". Rule 4: nobody dies.

**Clues visible before Ren says it:** the room is a circle; Rule 4 says "two **or more**", not "two"; Ren solved Game 2 by rereading one word ("Then I read rule two again").

**What it requires:** not one extra vote anywhere. That means not one token added, and every ballot naming the right person. Ren's safeguard against wrong names: show your card to the person you named, then put it in the slot. That leaves one thing nobody can check: **tokens in the slot.**

### Riku's one token, case by case
Only one person has a hidden token. It can only go on its holder's own card, and it can never land on Gō's name, because only Riku's card names Gō.

| If the hidden token… | Riku adds 0 | Riku adds 1 (what he did) | Riku adds 2 or more |
|---|---|---|---|
| goes on Riku's name | Riku 2 → **Riku dies** | Riku 2, Gō 2 → tie, nobody dies | Gō 3 or more → **Gō dies** |
| goes on someone else (Y) | Y 2 → **Y dies** | Y 2, Gō 2 → tie, nobody dies | Gō 3 or more → **Gō dies** |
| isn't used | everyone 1 → nobody dies | Gō 2 → **Gō dies** | Gō 3 or more → **Gō dies** |

Exactly 1 is the smallest amount that guarantees Riku's survival in every case. It is also the only amount under which a used hidden token kills nobody; with 2 or more, Gō dies in every case. Its price is Gō, but only if the class stays honest. That's the dark core of the game: **honesty kills Gō, and revenge would have saved him.**

## 7. Reader questions, answered

1. **Why not vote for Momo, like Game 2's trick?** Rule 1 says "another player", and Game 2 established that Momo isn't a player. Aoi points it out (7.12).
2. **Why not abstain?** Rule 1: no ballot, and you die.
3. **Why can't Gō just take Riku's tokens?** Rule 5.
4. **Why not search everyone for the missing token?** Rule 5 says it couldn't be taken even if found, so a search only destroys trust. A coin is easy to hide.
5. **Why can't the class outvote Riku?** He can add 69 tokens: 70 votes against 18.
6. **Why does the circle work?** The proof in section 3: everyone has 1 vote, and 19 players tie.
7. **Why do pairs fail but a circle works?** Pairs need an even number. A circle works with any number.
8. **Couldn't someone write a different name than agreed?** Each pedestal has one card, and everyone shows theirs to the person they named before putting it in. The only thing no one can see is tokens.
9. **Why didn't Riku give his tokens away?** Fear: they're the only thing stopping 18 people who hate him. He also said he'd never walk into the next game with nothing (Game 2). He refuses on the page (8.18–8.19).
10. **Why did Riku go last?** So nobody could react to what he did. It's a visible clue (8.22).
11. **Why exactly one token?** The case table in section 5.
12. **Why did the token hurt Gō and not someone Riku hates?** A token only adds to the name on your own card. Riku's card had to name the person on his left, and Gō chose to stand there.
13. **Why didn't Riku tell anyone about his token?** He trusts numbers, not people ("I trusted a number", Game 1). Going last means no one can react.
14. **Why didn't Kota use the token?** Saki's "He's still one of us", plus certain exposure: only his card named Riku.
15. **Why didn't Kota confess he had it?** Confessing means admitting he planned to kill Riku, and Rule 5 lets him keep it anyway.
16. **Did Riku lie?** No. He never agreed to add no tokens (8.21–8.22). That's a visible clue.
17. **How did Ren work out what happened?** Only Riku's card named Gō, so Gō's second vote had to be a token on Riku's card. Riku shows 68.
18. **Why is Rule 4 kind when Game 1 punished ties?** Different game, different rules. Haruto assumes ties are deadly; Aoi reads the actual rule (7.23–7.24). Why every game hides a way out stays an open question (section 9).
19. **How does Gō "die"?** The same way as Saki: lights out, then gone. Nothing new is revealed.
