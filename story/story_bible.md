These rules take priority over all other instructions.

PART 2 — CORE IDENTITY OF THE MANGA

Title: DEAD MAN’S ALGORITHM

Chapter 1 title: 12:17

Genre: Psychological thriller, survival games, mystery, catastrophe, suspense and character drama.

Primary setting: A Japanese high school, followed by a mysterious underground facility.

Central event: A meteor strikes at approximately 12:17 PM. Twenty students from Class 3-B awaken in an unknown facility.

Core premise: The students face an unknown system that forces them into survival games. Their personal information is already known to the system, their teacher is missing, and the condition of Earth is unknown.

Momo introduces itself as the administrator of DEAD MAN’S ALGORITHM.

The first announced game is GAME 01 — THE GLASS AUCTION.

The broader plan has been described as a sequence of 20 survival games. However, only the first game's rules are confirmed in the recovered material. Do not fabricate the remaining games and pretend they already exist in canon.

The story must function on two levels:

1. Immediate survival: the students must understand the rules, make decisions, and face consequences.
2. Long-term mystery: the meteor, facility, surveillance, personal data, Momo, and the system's purpose raise questions that have not yet been answered.

Do not allow the survival games to become disconnected episodes. Every event should contribute to the characters, their relationships, or the larger mystery.

PART 5 — GAME 01: THE GLASS AUCTION

Exact established rules

Rule 01: The highest unique bid wins the key.

Rule 02: All spent tokens are permanently lost.

Rule 03: If two or more players submit the same highest bid, every player tied at that amount is eliminated.

Rule 04: If the highest bid is not unique, the next-highest unique bid wins.

Rule 05: If all players leave all tokens unspent when time expires, everyone is executed.

Do not rewrite these rules for dramatic convenience.

Game parameters

- Players: 20.
- Each player starts with 100 tokens.
- Time limit: 10 minutes.
- Objective: Obtain the exit key.
- The exit key is the visible prize.
- Tokens are finite, and spending them has lasting consequences.
- A tied highest bid creates an elimination condition.
- If nobody spends any tokens before time expires, everyone is executed.

Game logic requirements

Before writing the resolution, build a complete private game-state table that records:

- Each player's submitted bid.
- Each player's starting tokens.
- Tokens spent.
- Tokens remaining.
- Whether the bid is unique.
- Whether the player is eliminated.
- The qualifying winning bid, if any.
- The exact point at which the game concludes.

Every outcome must follow the established rules.

Do not decide the winner first and reverse-engineer the bids afterward.

Do not grant Ren information about classmates' bids unless the rules or established events provide that information.

Do not let Ren read minds or predict every player's exact decision without evidence.

A strategy should emerge from information that Ren can observe, reason about or reasonably infer.

Unresolved auction mechanics

The displayed rules do not fully specify every possible edge case.

In particular, establish or ask me to confirm:

- How bids are submitted and whether they are public or hidden.
- Whether zero-token bids are permitted.
- Whether bids must be whole-number amounts.
- Whether players may communicate or coordinate bids.
- How the interaction between Rule 03 and Rule 04 is administered in an actual round.
- Whether multiple tied bid levels are handled simultaneously or sequentially.
- What precisely happens after a player is described as eliminated.
- What happens if no unique qualifying bid remains.
- Whether winning the key allows one person or all surviving players to exit.
- What constitutes a valid winning bid if a player cannot cover their declared amount.

These are unresolved design questions, not established rules.

Do not insert answers into the official canon without my approval. Preserve the exact five displayed rules as written while keeping the mechanics audit separate.

If you interpret Rule 03 and Rule 04 operationally, state your interpretation explicitly and mark it as provisional until I approve it.

PART 6 — SURVIVAL-GAME DESIGN RULES FOR THE ENTIRE SERIES

The planned series consists of 20 survival games, but do not invent the list of future games.

For each future game I approve, use the following development process.

A. Rules before drama

Write the complete rules before planning the solution.

Define:

- Objective.
- Participants.
- Starting resources.
- Time limit.
- Actions allowed.
- Actions forbidden.
- Win condition.
- Loss condition.
- Elimination condition.
- Tie-breaking conditions.
- Any penalties.
- Whether the game ends immediately or at a deadline.
- What information is visible to each player.

No critical mechanic may exist only in the author's mind.

B. Fair-play solution

The game must have a logically defensible resolution.

Before writing the chapter, test the rules against:

- Obvious strategies.
- Alternative strategies.
- Cooperation.
- Betrayal.
- Ties.
- Edge cases.
- Exploits.
- Attempts to avoid participating.
- Attempts to exploit ambiguous wording.

If an unintended loophole exists, resolve it by revising the rules before the game starts, not by introducing an emergency rule afterward.

Do not make every puzzle solvable by the same trick.

C. Foreshadowing

Every major solution must be supported by clues that appear before it is revealed.

A reader should have a fair chance to understand the solution retrospectively.

Do not:

- Hide essential information from the reader without a deliberate narrative justification.
- Introduce a new property of an object at the moment it becomes convenient.
- Give the protagonist a sudden ability or revelation that solves the problem.
- Reveal that a previously stated rule meant something completely different.
- Introduce a hidden condition only after a character loses.

A clue can be subtle without being meaningless.

D. Death and elimination

Do not kill characters arbitrarily.

Every elimination or death must follow the game rules and the actual sequence of events.

Maintain a permanent record of:

- Alive characters.
- Eliminated characters.
- Confirmed deaths.
- Injuries.
- Remaining tokens or other resources.
- Knowledge gained.
- Alliances and broken agreements.

Never bring back a dead character without an established explanation.

Do not confuse “eliminated,” “dead” and “executed” unless the game's rules explicitly establish their relationship.

E. Stakes and character psychology

Characters should not all react identically.

Under pressure, some may:

- Cooperate.
- Panic.
- Freeze.
- Lie.
- Become selfish.
- Protect a friend.
- Attempt to control the group.
- Make a rational decision that produces an emotionally difficult result.

Each reaction must fit the character and circumstances.

Do not force a betrayal or death purely to shock the reader.

F. Ren's reasoning

Use a consistent reasoning chain:

1. Observation.
2. Relevant evidence.
3. Hypothesis.
4. Alternative explanations.
5. Test or deduction.
6. Decision.
7. Consequence.

Let readers follow the reasoning. Do not rely on unexplained statements such as “Ren understood everything.”

Ren can be mistaken. When he is mistaken, let the mistake have believable consequences.

G. Every game must feel different

Do not reuse the same basic puzzle with different names.

When future games are approved, distinguish their strategic structures, resource systems, social pressures and decision types.

However, do not add any specific future game to the canon until its concept and rules have been established.
